PAID MEDIA · MEASUREMENT

Google Ads conversion tracking: what to verify before optimizing campaigns

Campaigns can generate clicks without generating meaningful business. Before making optimization decisions, confirm that your measurement reflects the actions you actually value: qualified enquiries, calls, booked appointments, or completed purchases.

Start with the customer action

List the steps that indicate intent, then separate high-value outcomes from softer signals. A submitted form may be valuable, but a page view or button click is usually only an early interaction. Use clear event names and verify that each important action fires once, on the right page or interaction.

Check the path from ad to outcome

Test landing pages, forms, call links, and thank-you states on mobile and desktop. Confirm that campaign parameters are retained and that analytics and advertising platforms receive consistent event data. Where sales happen later, a process for sharing qualified-lead or revenue outcomes can make reporting more useful.

  • Remove duplicate or outdated conversion actions.
  • Check consent, cross-domain journeys, and form completion behavior.
  • Review lead quality alongside cost per lead and conversion volume.

Once the signal is reliable, campaign changes become easier to interpret. Measurement doesn’t guarantee a better result by itself, but it gives the team a sounder basis for deciding what to test next.

SEO · LOCAL VISIBILITY

Local SEO: help nearby customers choose your business

Local search works best when customers can quickly understand what you do, where you work, and why your business is a suitable option. The goal is not to appear for every search. It is to be clear and credible for the searches that match your services and locations.

Make the basics accurate and useful

Keep your business name, contact information, service areas, and opening details consistent across the places customers use to evaluate you. Build service pages that answer real local questions instead of publishing thin pages that only swap a city name.

Earn trust through helpful detail

Explain your process, service boundaries, qualifications, and what customers should expect. Encourage genuine customer feedback and respond thoughtfully. Use structured information where it helps search engines understand your business, but make the page useful to people first.

  • Review local profiles and business details for accuracy.
  • Match each important service to a helpful page.
  • Track calls, enquiries, and qualified visits—not visibility alone.

Local SEO is ongoing. Review what people search, which pages assist their decision, and where enquiries come from; then improve the most useful gaps.

AUTOMATION · OPERATIONS

Business automation: keep every enquiry moving

When enquiries arrive across forms, calls, social channels, and email, the handoffs can become the weak point. Useful automation creates a dependable next step: capture the request, route it to the right person, and make follow-up visible—while leaving people in control of customer conversations.

Map the workflow before choosing tools

Start by writing down how an enquiry moves today: where it arrives, what information is needed, who responds, and what happens if there is no reply. Identify repeated tasks, duplicate entry, and missed handoffs before building automation around them.

Automate the routine; keep judgment with people

Connect forms and CRM records, use clear ownership rules, create reminders, and standardize useful status updates. Add customer-support automation only where it helps people get a timely response and provides a clear route to a human when the request needs judgment.

  • Keep data fields purposeful and limit unnecessary collection.
  • Test exceptions, duplicate records, and failed integrations.
  • Monitor response time, follow-up completion, and handoff quality.

Begin with one high-friction workflow, review how it performs, and then expand. A smaller system that your team understands is more useful than a complex automation nobody trusts.
